LAW No. 2025-9 of May 21, 2025
Regulating Employment Contracts and Prohibiting Labor Subcontracting

Preamble
Adopted by the Representatives of the People and promulgated by the President of the Republic.

CHAPTER I — EMPLOYMENT CONTRACTS

ARTICLE 1 — General Reform of the Labor Code Provisions

The provisions of Articles 6-2, 6-3, 6-4, 17, and the first paragraph of Article 94-2 of the Labor Code are repealed and replaced as follows:

---

ARTICLE 6-2 (NEW) — Nature of Employment Contracts
An employment contract is presumed to be concluded for an indefinite duration (permanent contract).

---

ARTICLE 6-3 (NEW) — Probation Period
An employment contract may include a probation period not exceeding six (6) months, renewable once for the same duration.

Either party may terminate the contract during the probation period by giving written notice at least fifteen (15) days before the end of the probation period.

If the contract is terminated during the probation period, any subsequent employment relationship between the parties shall be based on a permanent contract without probation.

---

ARTICLE 6-4 (NEW) — Fixed-Term Contracts
Fixed-term employment contracts are prohibited except in exceptional cases, including:
- Temporary increase in workload or services
- Temporary replacement of an absent employee or one whose contract is suspended
- Seasonal work or activities that, by nature, cannot be performed under a permanent contract

Employees hired under fixed-term contracts enjoy the same rights, benefits, and protections as permanent employees performing the same work.

They also have priority for permanent positions with the same employer.

A fixed-term contract must be written. If not written, or if it does not specify duration or legal justification, it is considered a permanent contract.

No probation period is allowed in fixed-term contracts.

---

ARTICLE 17 (NEW) — Continuation of Work After Contract Expiry
If an employee continues working after the expiration of a fixed-term contract, the contract automatically becomes a permanent contract.

The employee retains all seniority rights and is not subject to a probation period.

---

ARTICLE 94-2 (NEW — FIRST PARAGRAPH)
An employment contract may be concluded as either fixed-term or permanent for part-time work, in accordance with the provisions of the Labor Code.


CHAPTER II — PROHIBITION OF LABOR SUBCONTRACTING

ARTICLE 2 — Legal Repeal and Replacement
The provisions of Title II of Book One of the Labor Code are repealed and replaced by the following:

TITLE II — SERVICE PROVIDERS AND WORK CONTRACTING COMPANIES

---

ARTICLE 28 (NEW) — Prohibition of Labor Subcontracting
Labor subcontracting is strictly prohibited.

Labor subcontracting refers to any agreement between a labor leasing company and a client company whereby workers are hired out and placed under the control of the client company.

Cleaning and security activities are also considered forms of labor subcontracting when they involve worker leasing.

---

ARTICLE 29 (NEW) — Penalties
Any person who violates Article 28 is considered to have committed an offense.

- Individuals are punishable by a fine of 10,000 dinars.
- Legal entities are subject to a doubled fine.
- Company representatives or directors involved in illegal hiring are also fined 10,000 dinars.

In case of repeat offense:
- The offender is subject to imprisonment from 3 to 6 months.

---

ARTICLE 30 (NEW) — Service and Work Contracts
Employers may conclude written contracts with service or work-providing companies.

The first party is called the “client company” and the second the “service provider.”

Service provision includes specialized technical or professional work that:
- is not part of the core permanent activity of the client company
- does not involve worker supervision by the client company

---

ARTICLE 30 BIS — Prohibited Contracts
Any service or work contract that violates Article 30 is strictly prohibited.

---

ARTICLE 30 TER — Working Conditions and Responsibility
If no sector-specific wage system exists for the service provider:
- the wage system of the client company applies

The client company is responsible for:
- working conditions
- hygiene and safety rules
- working hours
- night work
- protection of women, children, and disabled workers
- weekly rest and public holidays

This applies when work is performed in the client’s premises, warehouses, or sites.

The service provider must provide proof of salary payment and social security contributions within 7 days of due date.

---

ARTICLE 30 QUATER — Financial Guarantee
The service provider must provide a financial guarantee to cover:
- employee salaries
- social security contributions

If the guarantee is insufficient:
- the client company becomes responsible for payment

Implementation details are set by ministerial decree.

---

ARTICLE 30 QUINQUIES — Direct Legal Action
Employees, the National Social Security Fund, and the National Health Insurance Fund may directly claim unpaid amounts from the client company.

---

ARTICLE 30 SEXIES — Joint Liability
When multiple parties are involved in a labor relationship:
- they are jointly and severally liable for obligations under labor law.


CHAPTER III — MISCELLANEOUS PROVISIONS

ARTICLE 3 — Penalties for Violations
A new Article 234 ter is added to the Labor Code:

Any person violating Article 6-4 (paragraph 1) and Article 30 bis shall be punished with a fine ranging from 100 to 300 dinars per affected worker.

Total fines may not exceed 10,000 dinars.

---

ARTICLE 4 — Repeals
The following provisions are repealed:
- Articles 28 and 29 referred to in Article 234 of the Labor Code
- Article 30 referred to in Article 234 bis of the Labor Code

---

ARTICLE 5 — Free Zone Law Repeal
Article 23 of Law No. 92-81 dated August 3, 1992 (Free Economic Zones) is repealed.

---

CHAPTER IV — TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

ARTICLE 6 — Transformation of Fixed-Term Contracts
Fixed-term contracts that do not fall under the exceptional cases defined in Article 6-4 are automatically converted into permanent contracts.

This applies regardless of contract expiry dates or completion of assigned work.

This rule applies to:
- contracts still in force
- contracts terminated after March 14, 2025

Employee seniority acquired under such contracts is fully recognized, provided the employment relationship was continuous and not interrupted for more than one year.

---

ARTICLE 7 — Probation Period Adjustment
Probation periods in contracts signed before this law remain valid only if they are shorter than six months.

---

ARTICLE 8 — Regularization of Subcontracted Workers
Workers employed under illegal labor subcontracting, as defined in Article 28, and permanent staff in public institutions, are considered directly employed by the client company from the date this law enters into force.

Their seniority is preserved, provided the employment relationship was continuous and not interrupted for more than one year.

---

ARTICLE 9 — Contract Termination and Compensation
Termination of fixed-term contracts between March 6, 2024 and the entry into force of this law results in automatic employment regularization if the employment duration reached four years or more.

If the employer fails to comply, the employee is entitled to compensation equal to two months of salary per year of seniority.

Claims for compensation expire one year after the law enters into force.

---

ARTICLE 10 — Compliance Deadline
Companies operating under Article 30 must regularize their legal status within three months of the law’s entry into force.

---

FINAL PROVISION
This law shall be published in the Official Gazette of the Republic of Tunisia and enforced as state law.

Tunis, May 21, 2025
President of the Republic: Kais Saied
